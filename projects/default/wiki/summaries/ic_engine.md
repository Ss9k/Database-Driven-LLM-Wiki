## Summary

An Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) is a heat engine that converts the chemical energy stored in fuel into mechanical work by burning the fuel inside a combustion chamber. This process involves expanding gases pushing a piston, which then transfers motion via a connecting rod to a crankshaft, generating rotational movement.

A typical four-stroke petrol engine operates through intake, compression, power, and exhaust stages. During intake, an air-fuel mixture enters the cylinder; it's then compressed before a spark plug ignites it, driving the piston downward in the power stroke. Finally, burnt gases are expelled. Diesel engines differ by compressing only air, with the high temperature causing self-ignition when diesel fuel is injected, eliminating the need for a spark plug.

These engines are widely utilized in automobiles, generators, and agricultural machinery. While offering advantages like high power density and quick refueling, their disadvantages include greenhouse gas emissions, noise, vibration, and dependence on fossil fuels.
