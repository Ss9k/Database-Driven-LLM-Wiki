# ic_engine

## Summary

Internal combustion engines convert chemical energy from fuel into mechanical work through combustion within a chamber. The engine's components, including cylinders, pistons, and valves, work together to facilitate this process. In a typical four-stroke petrol engine, air and fuel are drawn in, compressed, ignited, and then expelled as exhaust. Diesel engines, on the other hand, compress only air before injecting fuel, which ignites due to high temperatures. These engines are widely used in various applications, including automobiles, ships, and industrial equipment, due to their high power density and mature manufacturing technology. However, they also have drawbacks, such as greenhouse gas emissions, noise, and dependence on fossil fuels, highlighting the need for alternative energy sources and more efficient engine designs. Major manufacturers and industries rely on these engines, driving innovation and development in the field.
